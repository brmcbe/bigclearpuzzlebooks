Sudoku strategies: how to solve easy, medium and hard sudoku

Eight solving strategies, each shown on a big, clear example grid. Start with the first two: they solve every easy puzzle. Every puzzle in our books can be solved with these eight, with no guessing.

Easy puzzles: two strategies

1. Only one place for a number

Every box needs a 5. Find the 5s already in the puzzle and follow their rows and columns (shaded). They rule out two rows and one column of the top-left box, and the 8 fills another square. Only one square is left, so the 5 goes there.

2. Only one number fits

Pick an empty square and look at its row, its column and its box (shaded). Together they already hold 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8. The only number missing is 9, so 9 goes in that square.

Helpful habits

  • Start with the number you see most often in the puzzle.
  • Check one number at a time across all nine boxes.
  • Use a pencil, and keep an eraser handy.
  • Stuck? Switch to a different number or a different box.

Medium puzzles: pencil marks and pairs

Pencil marks

Write the numbers that could go in each empty square in small print in the corner. When a strategy rules a number out, cross it off. When only one is left, that's your answer. Fill them in one box at a time, and each time you write in an answer, erase that number from the pencil marks in its row, column and box.

3. Naked pair

Two squares in this row can only be 2 or 7 (outlined). One will be 2 and the other 7, so no other square in the row can be 2 or 7. Cross them off.

4. Hidden pair

In this row, 3 and 6 each fit in only two squares, and they're the same two squares (outlined). Those squares must hold the 3 and the 6, so cross off every other number in them.

5. Pointing pair

Pencil marks for 4 are shown. In the top-left box, the 4 in the third column rules out that column, so this box's 4 must go in the middle row, in one of the two outlined squares. Either way, the 4 is in that row, so cross 4 off the rest of the row.

It works the other way too: if, inside one row or column, a number's only spots all sit in the same box, cross that number off the rest of that box.

Hard puzzles: triples and wings

6. Naked triple

Three squares share just three numbers between them: 2, 5 and 8 (outlined). Those three squares will use up the 2, the 5 and the 8, so cross 2, 5 and 8 off the rest of the row. It works even when a square shows only two of the three numbers.

Hidden triple: if three numbers each fit in only the same three squares of a row, column or box, those squares must hold those three numbers. Cross off everything else in them.

7. X-Wing

Pencil marks for 7 are shown. In the 2nd row and the 7th row, 7 fits in only two squares, and they line up in the same two columns (outlined). However the 7s fall, they use up both columns, so cross 7 off everywhere else in those two columns.

Swordfish: the X-Wing idea with three rows and three columns. You'll rarely need it.

8. XY-Wing

Square A can only be 3 or 7. If A is 3, then B must be 9. If A is 7, then C must be 9. Either way, B or C is a 9. Square D sees both B and C, so D can't be 9. Cross the 9 off D. Most hard puzzles in our books need this one.

When you're stuck

  • Go back to strategies 1 and 2. They find most numbers, even in hard puzzles.
  • Make sure every empty square has up-to-date pencil marks.
  • Take a break. Fresh eyes spot the next number.
  • Peek at one square in the solution, then carry on.

Questions

What is the best strategy for solving sudoku?

Start with the two simple strategies: look for the only place a number can go in a box, row or column, and for a square where only one number fits. They solve every easy puzzle and find most numbers in harder ones.

Do you ever need to guess in sudoku?

Not in a well-made puzzle. Every puzzle in our books can be solved with the eight strategies on this page, with no guessing.

What strategies do hard sudoku puzzles need?

Pencil marks plus pairs, triples, the X-Wing and the XY-Wing. The XY-Wing is the one most hard puzzles in our books need.

What are pencil marks in sudoku?

Small numbers written in the corner of an empty square to show which numbers could still go there. You cross them off as strategies rule them out.
